## Interpretation

At every budget from 1 to 256 simulator tests, the Joint Tuner solves significantly more tasks than Nelder-Mead from a cold start. The gap peaks at +49 pp at B = 4-8 (76 vs 27 at B = 8, McNemar p < 1e-12), and every one of those nine p-values stays below 0.01 after a Holm correction across the budgets. On the tasks both methods eventually solve, the Joint Tuner needs a median of 1 test versus 19.5 (Wilcoxon p < 1e-9), and its area under the log-budget curve is 0.77 vs 0.43 (diff +0.34, 95% CI +0.26 to +0.42). The advantage is sample efficiency, not final capability: at the full 600-test budget the optimizer alone solves 93 vs our 90 (p = 0.25, not significant), and our model plateaus after B = 256. Against GPT-6.1 Terra, both the Joint Tuner (90 vs 89, p = 1.0, CI -4 to +6 pp) and the fine-tuned 9B best-of-32 (89 vs 89) are statistically indistinguishable, so the honest claim is that a 9B model plus a simulator matches GPT-6.1 Terra, not that it beats it. With n = 100, this design can only detect differences of about 5-6 pp or more.

Caveats: `repair_v3_n8_stab` (90) and the trace's @600 column (90) disagree on 2 tasks. `coldstart_150x3` (84) is a separate, differently budgeted optimizer run from the trace's cold arm (93) and disagrees with it on 9 tasks. The `second_order_unstable` category is unsolved by every method at low budget and is the main remaining failure mode.
